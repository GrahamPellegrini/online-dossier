DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is

Three common myths

Myth

“It knows everything.”

It learned patterns from text. There is no list of facts inside to look up.

Myth

“It searches the internet for the answer.”

A chatbot writes from what it learned. Some tools can also search, but that is an extra feature.

Myth

“It thinks like a person.”

It predicts likely words. Very useful, but different from thinking.

DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is · the brain

Start with your brain

  • About 86 billion neurons.
  • Each one connects to thousands of others.
  • A neuron collects signals. If they are strong enough, it fires and passes a signal on.

Neuron count: Azevedo et al. (2009), Journal of Comparative Neurology.

AI Tools for Work and StudyGraham Pellegrini | University of Malta DegreePlusLecture 1 · 16 / 38
DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is · learning

Learning = stronger connections

  • Practise something, and the connections you use get stronger.
  • That is why the hundredth bike ride is easier than the first.
  • Neuroscientists sum it up as: neurons that fire together, wire together.

Idea from Donald Hebb, The Organization of Behavior (1949).

DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is · the copy

An artificial neuron: a simple maths copy

  • It takes in numbers.
  • Each input has a weight: how much it matters.
  • It adds them up and passes a result on.
  • The weights play the role of connection strength.

DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is · the network

Connect lots of them: a neural network

  • Layers of artificial neurons pass numbers forward.
  • Big models have hundreds of billions of weights. Meta’s open model Llama 3.1 has 405 billion.
  • Your brain has roughly 100 trillion connections: still far more.

Meta, The Llama 3 Herd of Models (2024). Synapse count is a common estimate; figures vary.

DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is · training

How it learns: guess, check, adjust

1

Show it some text with the next word hidden.

2

It guesses the missing word.

3

Compare the guess with the real word.

4

Nudge the weights so the right word becomes a little more likely.

Repeat this trillions of times. Nobody types in the rules: the patterns come from the examples.

DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is · learning

A bit like a child learning a language

A child
  • Hears thousands of sentences.
  • Nobody teaches grammar rules first.
  • Starts to predict what people will say.
An AI model
  • Reads trillions of words.
  • Nobody programs the rules.
  • Learns which words tend to follow which.

One big difference: a child also sees, touches and lives in the world. The model only has text.

Scale

Meta’s Llama 3 learned from about 15 trillion pieces of text.

Reading non-stop at 250 words a minute, 24 hours a day, a person would need roughly 85,000 years to read that much.

Meta AI, Llama 3 announcement (April 2024): over 15 trillion tokens. Reading time is a rough estimate at about 0.75 words per token.

DGP0959 | AI Tools for Work and StudyWhat AI is · limits

Why it sounds so sure of itself

What it is trained to do

Produce text that is likely and sounds right.

What it is not built to do

Know whether a statement is true.

So it is excellent at drafting, explaining and rewording. It still needs you to check facts, numbers and sources.

One more thing: it does not read words

AI splits text into tokens. Common words are one piece; rarer words are cut into chunks.

English: 9 tokens
Unbelievable results at the University of Malta
Maltese: 2 words, 6 tokens
Pastizzi tal-irkotta

Split with OpenAI’s o200k tokenizer, used by recent ChatGPT models.
